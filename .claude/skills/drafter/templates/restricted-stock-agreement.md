# Restricted Stock Purchase Agreement (RSPA) Framework

## Standard sections

### 1. Parties and recitals
- `[TAILOR]` Company and purchaser (founder/co-founder) identification
- Recital that the company wishes to issue shares subject to vesting and repurchase rights
- Reference to the equity incentive plan (if applicable)

### 2. Purchase and sale of shares
- `[TAILOR]` Number of shares, price per share, total purchase price
- Payment method (cash, promissory note, IP assignment as consideration)
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` If using IP assignment as consideration, the valuation must be defensible. This affects 409A and tax treatment.

### 3. Vesting schedule
- `[TAILOR]` Vesting commencement date, total vesting period, cliff period
- Standard: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, monthly thereafter
- `[TAILOR]` Define what "continuous service" means (employment, consulting, board service)

### 4. Company repurchase right
- Unvested shares are subject to repurchase at the original purchase price upon termination of service
- Repurchase right lapses as shares vest
- Company has [30-90] days after termination to exercise repurchase right
- `[TAILOR]` Specify the repurchase window

### 5. Acceleration provisions (if applicable)
- **Single trigger:** All or portion of unvested shares vest upon change of control
- **Double trigger:** Acceleration requires both change of control AND termination
- `[TAILOR]` Choose single, double, or no acceleration. Specify percentage (full or partial).
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Acceleration provisions affect the company's attractiveness to acquirers. Single trigger is founder-friendly but may concern investors.

### 6. Transfer restrictions
- Shares may not be transferred without company consent
- Right of first refusal on any proposed transfer
- Company may assign repurchase and ROFR rights to the company or its assignees
- Lock-up agreement for IPO (if applicable)

### 7. Section 83(b) election
- Purchaser acknowledges the opportunity to file an 83(b) election with the IRS within 30 days of the purchase date
- **CRITICAL DEADLINE:** The 83(b) election must be filed within 30 days. This deadline cannot be extended.
- Include a copy of the 83(b) election form as an exhibit
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Failure to timely file 83(b) can result in significant adverse tax consequences. This is one of the most important compliance items for founders receiving restricted stock.

### 8. Tax matters
- Purchaser is responsible for all tax consequences
- Company has no obligation to advise on tax matters
- Withholding obligations if applicable

### 9. Representations and warranties
- Purchaser is acquiring shares for investment, not resale
- Purchaser understands the shares are restricted securities
- Purchaser has had opportunity to ask questions
- Purchaser can afford the total loss of the investment
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` These representations are required for securities law compliance

### 10. Legends
- Securities law legend on stock certificates or book entries
- Vesting and repurchase legend
- `[TAILOR]` Include any additional legends required by state law

### 11. General provisions
- Governing law — `[TAILOR]` to state of incorporation
- Entire agreement
- Amendment (written, signed by both parties)
- Severability

### 12. Exhibits
- Exhibit A: Vesting schedule
- Exhibit B: 83(b) election form
- Exhibit C: Spouse/partner consent (if applicable in community property states)
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Community property states (CA, TX, WA, etc.) may require spousal consent for stock transfers

## Key warnings
- The 83(b) election deadline is non-negotiable — generate an attorney flag if there's any risk of missing it
- Vesting terms should be consistent with any existing stockholder agreements or investor expectations
- If the company has existing investors, check whether the investment documents require investor consent for new stock issuances
