# Confidential Information and Invention Assignment Agreement (CIIA) Framework

## Standard sections

### 1. Confidentiality obligations
- Definition of confidential information
- Obligation to protect during and after employment
- Return of materials upon termination

### 2. Invention assignment
- All inventions related to company's business, made during employment, are assigned to company
- Obligation to disclose inventions
- Assistance with patent applications
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Several states (CA, DE, IL, KS, MN, NC, WA) have statutory carve-outs protecting employee inventions made on their own time without company resources. The agreement must include the required statutory notice.

### 3. Prior inventions
- Employee lists prior inventions that are excluded
- `[TAILOR]` Attach schedule for employee to complete

### 4. Non-solicitation of employees (if applicable)
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Enforceability varies by state

### 5. Non-competition (if applicable)
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Many states limit or prohibit non-competes. CA bans them entirely. FTC has proposed federal restrictions. Only include if enforceable in the employee's state AND the company actually needs it.
- `[TAILOR]` Must be narrowly tailored to be enforceable where permitted

### 6. Remedies
- Acknowledgment of irreparable harm
- Right to injunctive relief

### 7. General provisions
- Governing law
- Severability (critical for non-competes — if one provision is unenforceable, others survive)
- Entire agreement

## Required state-specific notices

The following states require specific language notifying employees of their rights regarding inventions:
- California (Labor Code § 2870)
- Delaware (19 Del. C. § 805)
- Illinois (765 ILCS 1060/2)
- Kansas (K.S.A. § 44-130)
- Minnesota (Minn. Stat. § 181.78)
- North Carolina (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 66-57.1)
- Washington (RCW § 49.44.140)

`[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Verify current statutory text — these provisions are occasionally amended.
