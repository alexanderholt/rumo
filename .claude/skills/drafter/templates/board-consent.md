# Written Consent in Lieu of Meeting — Board of Directors Framework

## Standard sections

### 1. Header
- `[TAILOR]` Company legal name
- "Action by Written Consent of the Board of Directors"
- Date of consent
- "In lieu of a meeting, pursuant to [applicable statute]"
- `[TAILOR]` Delaware: DGCL § 141(f). Other states: cite applicable statute.

### 2. Recitals
- The board has determined it is in the best interest of the company to take the following actions
- `[TAILOR]` Brief context for why these actions are being taken

### 3. Resolutions
Each resolution follows the pattern:
- "RESOLVED, that [specific action] is hereby approved."
- "FURTHER RESOLVED, that [authorization for officers to execute]."

Common resolution types for early-stage startups:
- **Option grants:** Approve issuance of stock options under the equity incentive plan, specifying recipient, number of shares, exercise price, vesting schedule
- **Officer appointments:** Appoint or remove officers, specifying title and authority
- **Fundraising authorization:** Authorize the company to enter into SAFEs, convertible notes, or equity financing, specifying terms and maximum amounts
- **Bank account / corporate matters:** Open accounts, change signatories, authorize filings
- **Contractor/vendor approvals:** Approve material contracts above a threshold
- **Equity incentive plan adoption:** Adopt or amend the stock option plan, specifying share reserve

`[TAILOR]` Include only the resolutions relevant to the specific action being taken. Do not include boilerplate resolutions unrelated to the matter at hand.

### 4. General authority
- "FURTHER RESOLVED, that the officers of the Company are authorized to take all actions necessary to carry out the purposes of the foregoing resolutions."
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` This catch-all authorization should be appropriately scoped. Overly broad general authority can create issues.

### 5. Signature block
- Signature lines for each director
- Printed name and date for each
- `[TAILOR]` List all current directors. Consent requires unanimous written consent unless the certificate of incorporation provides otherwise (Delaware).

## Notes
- Written consents must be unanimous in Delaware unless the certificate of incorporation provides otherwise
- `[ATTORNEY REVIEW]` Verify whether the company's charter documents allow less-than-unanimous written consent
- File the executed consent with the corporate records
- Some actions (e.g., adopting or amending equity plans, authorizing new classes of stock) may also require stockholder approval
